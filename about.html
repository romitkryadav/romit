The Person Behind The Code

About Romit Kr Yadav

Independent creator, web developer, and someone who enjoys building things on the internet. 19 years old, from Bihta, Patna, Bihar.

Hello, I'm Romit

My name is Romit Kr Yadav. I'm 19 years old, based in Bihta, Patna, Bihar. I'm an independent creator, web developer, and someone who genuinely enjoys building things on the internet.

I'm interested in web development, SEO, online tools, websites, digital products, design, and learning how things work behind the scenes. I spend a lot of my time thinking about new ideas, building websites, testing different tools, studying search engines, and trying to understand what people actually need.

I didn't start with a team, a big investment, or a complete technical background. I started with curiosity and the desire to build something of my own. I learned most of these things by trying to build real projects, getting stuck, searching for solutions, making mistakes, and trying again. That's still how I learn today.

Why I Started Building Websites

My interest in websites started because I liked the idea that one person could create something on the internet and make it available to people anywhere in the world.

I found it interesting that a simple website could solve a small problem for thousands or even millions of people. Someone could search for something on Google, find a useful tool, use it for a few seconds, and leave with their problem solved. That idea really attracted me.

Instead of only consuming things on the internet, I wanted to understand how they were created. I started experimenting with websites, online tools, domains, hosting, design, APIs, and different technologies. I learned HTML and CSS, started working with JavaScript, experimented with APIs, learned about GitHub and deployment, worked with Cloudflare, and slowly became more comfortable with the different parts involved in creating a website.

My Journey

My journey began with no team, no large investment, and no perfect roadmap. I mainly had curiosity and a goal of creating useful things.

After completing my 12th board exams, I became more serious about learning websites, SEO, online businesses, and digital products. I started spending a lot of time researching how websites work, how Google finds and ranks pages, what people search for, and what kinds of tools people actually use.

I quickly realized that creating a website is only one part of the process. You can build a website that looks great and works perfectly, but that doesn't mean anyone will find it. That's when SEO became a major interest for me.

I started learning about keywords, search intent, competition, content, internal linking, technical SEO, indexing, structured data, page speed, and user experience. The more I learned, the more I realized that SEO isn't simply about putting keywords on a page — you have to understand what someone is actually looking for and create something that genuinely satisfies that search.

Building Online Tools

After spending more time researching search behavior, I noticed something simple: people search for solutions to very specific problems every day.

They want to resize an image.
They want to download something.
They want to check something.
They want to convert something.
They want quick information.
One tool, one job, done.

That became one of the main ideas behind many of my projects. My goal wasn't to build the most complicated software possible. I wanted to create tools that were simple, fast, easy to understand, and useful.

My Projects

One of my major projects is InstaDPdownload. The idea came from a simple problem — Instagram profile pictures were displayed in a small circular format, making it difficult to properly view them. So I built a tool that makes viewing and downloading Instagram profile pictures as simple as possible. Building it taught me far more than just creating the actual tool — SEO, content, user experience, performance, search rankings, branding, and how much work goes into maintaining a website after launch.

Some projects received traffic. Some didn't. Some ideas looked good when I started but didn't work the way I expected. I don't consider those failures useless — they taught me things that a successful project probably wouldn't have.

Learning From Failure

One of the biggest things I've learned is that having a good idea doesn't automatically make a good product.

Sometimes I thought a particular tool would get a lot of traffic, only to discover the competition was much stronger than expected. Sometimes I focused too much on building the tool and not enough on understanding the actual search intent. Sometimes a website worked technically but wasn't good enough from a user-experience perspective.

These experiences taught me to question my own assumptions. Before spending too much time building something now, I try to think about whether people actually need it, whether they are searching for it, how competitive it is, and whether I can create something genuinely useful.

My Philosophy

I don't believe you need to know everything before starting. If I had waited until I completely understood programming, SEO, design, hosting, APIs, and online businesses, I probably wouldn't have built much.

Instead, I started before I was ready and learned along the way. I've made plenty of mistakes. I've built things the wrong way. I've spent time on ideas that weren't worth it. But all of those experiences have helped me improve.

Learn → Build → Test → Fail → Fix → Improve → Repeat.

Today, I'm still learning and still building. I don't consider myself an expert, and I don't want to pretend that I know everything. For me, the goal isn't to look successful on the internet. The goal is to actually become better at what I do.

What's Next

My long-term goal is to create useful digital products — things that real people actually use. That could be a simple web tool, a larger web application, an Android app, or something I haven't even thought of yet.

I want to become a better developer, understand SEO at a much deeper level, build better products, and understand digital growth and business. I'm still at the beginning, and I have a lot left to learn. But that's also what makes the journey interesting.

"I started with curiosity. Then I started building. Building made me want to learn more. Learning made me want to build better things. And that's where I am today."

I'm Romit Kr Yadav. I'm still learning. I'm still building. And this is only the beginning.
